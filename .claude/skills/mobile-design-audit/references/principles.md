# Apple design principles as audit questions

Apple reintroduced eight design principles in the HIG in June 2026
(https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles).
Apple says there is no formula for them: they are tools to weigh competing
priorities. Leaning into one can cost another. Use the questions below on each
screen and write a finding when the answer is "no".

## 1. Purpose: make something meaningful

- Can you say in one sentence what this screen is for?
- Does every element serve that sentence? Each feature asks for time,
  attention, and trust. What can we remove?
- Is the most important feature the best-made one?
- Does the design solve the problem in a way that sets the app apart, or does
  it copy a generic pattern without reason?

## 2. Agency: let people do things their own way

- Does the screen take people straight to the task, without a forced path?
- Can a guided flow (onboarding, a wizard) be skipped or left at any step?
- Can people undo or go back after a mistake, without losing work?
- Is a confirmation used only before a big mistake that cannot be undone?
- Are there choices where a person would want one (units, order, what shows)?

## 3. Responsibility: act in people's best interest

- Is each permission asked at the moment of need, with a clear reason?
- Does the app collect only what it needs, and say why?
- How could this feature be misused, and who could be harmed?
- For AI features: what happens when the model is wrong? Are there previews,
  confirmations, or limits? Is a risky feature worth keeping at all?

## 4. Familiarity: build on what people know

- Do common actions use the symbols and words people already know (trash for
  delete, a share icon for share)? Is any familiar symbol used for something
  else?
- Do things that look the same behave the same everywhere?
- Is each action in the same place on every screen?
- Is feedback clear: what can I tap, what changed, did it work?

## 5. Flexibility: adapt to contexts and needs

- Does the screen work at the largest text size, with a screen reader, with
  reduced motion, with increased contrast, in dark mode, one-handed, outdoors
  in sunlight, and in a hurry (for example mid-workout)?
- Does it work on a small phone, a large phone, a foldable, and in landscape?
- Can people hide or reorder things they never use, where that matters?

## 6. Simplicity: be clear and direct

- Simple is not minimal. Is anything important hidden in a menu just to make
  the screen look clean?
- Is the most important item the most obvious one (order, spacing, contrast)?
- Are words plain and short? Is anything said twice?
- Could a number list be a small chart, or a long text a summary?
- Sometimes simple means adding context (time left, last saved). Is anything
  missing that people need to decide?

## 7. Craft: care about every detail

- Does every tap respond at once? Is scrolling smooth?
- Are icons, text, and edges aligned? Are spacing and radii from the tokens?
- Does the layout survive rotation, large text, and long words?
- Is the app current with the platform (new system components, gestures)?

## 8. Delight: make it human

- What feeling should the app give? Does the design support it?
- Are the defining moments (finishing a workout, a first log, an error) given
  care?
- Is anything decoration that slows the task? Delight is not confetti; it is
  the sum of the other seven principles done well.
