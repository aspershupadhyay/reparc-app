Warm-up · what already works

Keep these. They are rare.

Most rival apps can't make these claims. They are the base we build on, so nothing in this review asks you to remove them.

✓No account, no ads, no paywall

The market leaders lock basics behind subscriptions. Reparc gives you everything on day one.

✓Works with no signal

Basement gyms have no network. Every record saves on the phone first.

✓Guided workout with timers and voice

Set clock, rest clock and "three, two, one". Ramp-up rests are shorter, which fixes a common Hevy complaint.

✓Plans that decide for you

Five goals × three levels, with warm-ups, ramp-up sets and double progression.

✓Swap when the machine is taken

Same muscle, closest move first, and your logged sets stay.

✓Honest numbers

A missing value shows as "—", never 0. Estimates are marked. Export and delete everything.

The main lift · why would anyone switch

One sentence people repeat to friends

Hevy and Strong are logbooks: you decide, they record. Fitbod decides for you but costs a lot. MyFitnessPal tracks food but charges for its barcode scanner. No app does all three things below for free, privately, and with Indian food in it.

Proposed promise

"Tap start. Reparc tells you every weight, every rep, every rest. Free. Private. Works with no signal."

The one thing: you never have to think in the gym. Open the app, see today's workout, and follow it set by set. It picks next week's weight for you. Beginners stop feeling lost, and gym bros get progressive overload without spreadsheets. Most of the engine for this already exists (guided mode and double progression). It just isn't the first thing a new user sees.

For gym bros

PRs you can feel

Lifters train for the moment the bar moves more than last time. Reparc records PRs but doesn't celebrate them.

  • A full-screen PR moment with haptics: "New best: Bench 82.5 kg × 6"
  • A clean share card for Instagram stories, with no account needed
  • A weekly streak that forgives a missed day (rest is part of training)
For India

Protein from real Indian food

The food list has 84 items, mostly American (USDA). It has 2 dal entries and no paneer, soya chunks, idli, dosa, poha or rajma. Indian gym-goers struggle most with protein, and no workout app solves it.

  • About 300 Indian staples with katori, roti and piece sizes
  • A protein-first view: "78 of 115 g · add 2 eggs or 1 scoop whey"
  • "Same as yesterday" in one tap

Working sets · what feels unprofessional

Taste gaps, screen by screen

All screenshots are from your phone today, except the live workout screen, which I rendered from the app's code. Each problem has a severity: Fix first Before launch Polish

The body map looks like a painted bodysuit

Fix first

Colours are by body area, not by muscle. The lats, traps, upper back and lower back are one blue block. You can't see where one muscle ends.

Fix first

A tapped muscle barely changes. Selecting the lats turns them a slightly brighter blue on a blue back.

Before launch

The paint hides the form, so the body doesn't read as muscular, and it fills only about half the panel.

Fix: a neutral sculpted body with thin lines between muscles, and a lime glow on the one you tap. Best quality comes from a Blender pre-render, which is your open decision from earlier.

Home: clean, but it doesn't know you

Before launch

No first-run setup. A new user lands on someone else's plan and four empty food rings. Nobody asked their goal, days or gym, and "Good afternoon" has no name.

Before launch

Four dashes in rings look broken. An empty ring should invite an action ("Log breakfast") rather than show "—".

Polish

"0/6 plan" on a Saturday reads like failure on day one. Count from the day the plan started.

Fix: a three-question start (goal, days a week, where you train) that takes about 20 seconds, then "Your first workout: Push · 45 min". Retention research says long questionnaires lose users, so stop at three.

Analytics and plan: zeros and mixed numbers

Before launch

An empty week shows "0 · Same as before" four times. It feels dead. A new user needs a picture of what will appear: "Finish one workout to see your volume and PRs here."

Before launch

Numbers disagree. Home says 0/6 plan days, Analytics says 0 of 2 planned workouts, and the plan says "Mon Push · next" on a Saturday. One user, three counts.

Polish

No reward moment. Nothing celebrates a finished workout or a PR, and there's no streak and no share card.

Meal: too many numbers, too few foods

Fix first

84 foods, mostly American. A new Indian user searches "paneer", finds nothing, and leaves. Typing food in by hand is where food tracking dies.

Before launch

Seven numbers on one card (energy, protein, carbs, fibre, fat, sugar, sodium) is a dietitian's view. A gym-goer wants protein and calories first and the rest one tap away.

Polish

The "Add food" button covers the Breakfast pill, and the meal timeline sits below the fold.

Fix: protein-first card, Indian staples with household portions, a "Same as yesterday" button and recent foods at the top. A barcode scanner is a later decision (it needs an online food source).

Live workout: the best screen, still too busy

Before launch

Jargon in the most important place: "3 reps left", "3 × 6–10", "W" for warm-up, "Double progression with 1–2 reps left in the tank". Beginners won't understand these.

Polish

The body-part icons are coloured blobs, and they look homemade next to the clean list.

Fix: say "Stop when 3 more reps would be hard" once, with a small "?" for the term. Give warm-up sets a word instead of a letter. Use real muscle icons, made from the same body as the map.

Me: a developer's settings page

Before launch

Support opens GitHub. Normal people don't have GitHub accounts. Play also needs a support email.

Before launch

"AI connections · 4 data types chosen", "GNU AGPL v3.0", "MCP". These words are for engineers. Move them under an "Advanced" row.

Polish

No profile at the top (name, goal, current plan). Me should start with you, not with units.

Research · what users complain about elsewhere

Pain points we can win on

These come from reviews, surveys and comparison articles. Many sources are rival apps' blogs, so read the numbers as direction, not as exact facts.

60%of gym members worry most about not knowing how to use the machines

43% don't know where to begin, and 36% find asking for help the hardest part (PureGym survey, 800+ members).

Our answer: a "First day" plan with the photo, setup and seat height for every machine, so nobody has to ask.

PureGym via The Conversation

3–8%of users are still active on day 30

Most quit in the first two weeks. Long setup questionnaires, too many notifications and "no value felt" are the top reasons.

Our answer: value in the first minute (today's workout), a forgiving weekly streak, and a PR moment in week one.

AppsFlyer and Adjust via Sahha; Braze

12/15top fitness apps share personal data with third parties

Free apps share more than paid ones. People are getting more careful about their health data.

Our answer: we share nothing. Say it on the store page and on the first screen: "Your data never leaves this phone."

Surfshark via TechRadar

#1complaint in food tracking: the barcode scanner behind a paywall

MyFitnessPal moved it to Premium years ago and users are still angry. Its crowd-sourced food entries are often wrong.

Our answer: a checked, Indian-first food list, free forever. Never paywall the basics.

Kimola; Unstar review analysis

3 tapsper set is too many

"I don't want to spend more time logging than lifting." Users want last session's weights filled in.

Our answer: we already pre-fill. Next, make the whole set one tap: ✓ = did what was planned.

Reddit summary via PonteFuerte

Lifters want short rests on warm-ups and long rests on heavy sets.

Our answer: already built (60 s ramp-up rests). Mention it in the store listing.

No account at all.

Competition day · Google Play readiness

What stands between you and the store

Checked against the manifest, the build scripts, the handbook and Google's published rules. Target SDK 36 already meets the August 2026 requirement.

Blockers 7

Self-update permission

REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES and the in-app APK updater break Play's Device and Network Abuse policy. The Play build must leave them out and let Play deliver updates.

Not done
Voice clip licence

The voice clips come from ElevenLabs' free plan, which allows non-commercial use only. Re-make them on a paid plan or record new ones.

Not done
Privacy policy page

The website has a privacy section, but Play and Health Connect need a full policy at its own URL, and it must match the in-app link.

Not done
Health apps declaration and Data safety form

Each of the 5 Health Connect permissions needs a written reason in the Play Console.

Not done
App Bundle and signing

Release builds an APK, but Play needs an AAB. Without the key secrets, signing falls back to a debug key. Set up an upload key and Play App Signing.

Not done
Exact alarm permission

Play limits SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM to apps whose core job is alarms. Remove it or justify it, because the rest alert works without it.

Risk
Closed test, 12 testers, 14 days

Required for new personal developer accounts before production. Organisation accounts are exempt.

Test day · a real workout on your phone

What I tested on your realme

I ran a real Push workout through the warm-up and the first bench press sets, sent the app to the background during a rest, then discarded the workout so your history stays clean. I also set the phone's font to Very large, checked the main screens, and set it back to Default.

Two bugs that lose workout data

Fix first

A set ended by its timer drops the typed weight. I typed 30 kg into a ramp-up set. The set timer ran out, the app ticked the set as done on its own, and the 30 kg was gone, with reps empty too. The set is saved as done with no weight and no reps.

Fix first

The button changes under your finger. At the end of a rest, "Skip rest" turns into "Set done" as the next set starts on its own. One tap at 0:00 ticked the next set as done, again with no weight or reps. In a real gym this happens with sweaty hands and no time to check.

Before launch

"8 reps at 50%" with an empty kg box. A new user has no working weight yet, so 50% of what? The screen should ask once ("What can you lift for 8?") or offer the weight calculator.

Note: I typed the weight through Latch, which enters text the same way an accessibility tool does. Please try the first bug once by hand to confirm it. The second bug comes from the screen layout alone.

The live workout mostly works well

Works

Warm-up timers, rest timers and ramp-up sets counted down correctly and moved on by themselves. The ramp-up rest was 60 seconds, as designed.

Works

The finish sheet is clear: "7 of 29 sets done", save, save as incomplete, keep going, or discard. Discard asks again before deleting. This is the best-written screen in the app.

Polish

Small glitches: two dots overlap at the top of the timer ring. The start screen says "READY" over a clock that is already counting. The plan says "7 exercises" but the finish sheet says "12" because it counts warm-up moves.

Large text: mostly fine, two problems

Before launch

Home cuts off the food targets: "of 29…" and "of 2,1…". The one number the user needs is the one that's hidden.

Before launch

On Meal, "Add food" covers the Targets button, so it can't be tapped without scrolling.

Works

Titles, buttons and the workout screens grew without breaking. The tab bar labels still fit.

Also missing · bigger than one screen

Six things the first draft didn't cover

Licence

The app's licence page says GNU AGPL v3, but the code is in a private repository. Under AGPL, anyone who installs the app can ask for the full source code, so publishing on Play under AGPL means you're promising to open-source it. You own the code, so you can choose a different licence. Decide before launch.

Status: decision needed, and it can block launch.
Money

"Free, no paywall" is the strongest promise, but nothing says how the app pays for itself. Options that keep the promise: a one-time supporter purchase for extras (themes, advanced charts, never basics), tips, or a paid plan only for things that cost money to run (cloud sync, an AI coach).

Status: decide now. Adding a paywall later is what users hate about MyFitnessPal.
Search

Nobody searches the Play Store for "Reparc". The listing title should carry the words people type, for example "Reparc: Gym Workout & Protein Tracker". The first screenshot should show the promise in one line, not a settings screen.

Status: part of the store listing work.
Blind

The app has no usage numbers at all, so after launch you won't know whether people quit on day 2 or day 20. Opt-in counts like "workouts finished per week", with no health data in them, would answer that. Your handbook already keeps this kind of count separate from users' own progress data.

Status: decision needed.
iPhone

The iPhone build is unsigned. The App Store needs a paid Apple developer account (US$99 a year), plus its own review and privacy labels.

Status: later, once Android is live.
Hindi

Hindi or Hinglish wording would matter for Indian users. Also, "your data is safe" only holds if the phone's Google backup is on. The app should check that and say it plainly.

Status: after launch for Hindi. Before launch for the backup check.

Cool-down · decisions only you can make

Your calls before I start

Licence

Keep AGPL and publish the source code, or choose a licence that keeps the code private. This has to be settled before the store build.

How it makes money

A supporter purchase, tips, or a paid plan only for features that cost money to run. Never paywall the basics.

Who is the first user?

My pick: Indian gym-goers aged 18–30, beginner to intermediate. It decides the food list, the words and the store listing. The handbook still lists the target user as open.

How should the Play build differ from the GitHub build?

Your rule says no separate app variants, but Play bans the self-updater. Options: one Play-only build that drops the updater (same app ID), or stop the GitHub builds once Play is live.

Voice clips

Pay for an ElevenLabs plan that allows commercial use and re-make the clips, record a real voice, or ship without voice for launch.

Crash reports

None (most private, but you're blind to crashes), or opt-in reports with no health data in them.

Developer account

Personal (needs the 12-tester, 14-day closed test) or organisation (needs a D-U-N-S number, no closed test).

The body map

A Blender pre-rendered neutral body (best look) or an improved live 3D body. This is still open from our last chat.
